TU/e Final Bachelor Project

The interactive monitoring platform for urban nature.

Wildzoekers is a nature monitoring platform, wrapped in an immersive exploration game. Together with local residents, it maps the hidden biodiversity within intentionally wild urban spaces.

Why does this matter?

Wild nature in cities is unfortunately often overlooked, labeled as 'weeds' or seen as a lack of maintenance.

But it is precisely these untouched, rugged verges and spontaneous vegetation that are the source of urban biodiversity. Insects, birds, and small mammals find their indispensable food sources and shelters here.

How does Wildzoekers work?

Wildzoekers begin their journey of discovery with the striking physical insects on the street and end with actively monitoring all nature in the neighborhood.

1
Playful Discovery

Physical objects catch attention & scan

Physical elements in the neighborhood instantly draw the attention of passersby. Tangible 3D insects are hidden among the vegetation, inviting people to interact. Scanning the QR codes launches the game right away.

2
Education & Communication

Learn about nature through the platform

As soon as an insect is scanned, the mobile platform opens up. Here, residents instantly discover the true ecological value of this specific location. The platform transparently communicates why this rugged ecosystem is intentional and vital.

3
Community Participation

Actively monitor urban nature together

This is where the monitoring infrastructure truly comes alive. Residents transform into active citizen scientists by maintaining live wild animal counts and uploading photo observations, building a shared local database.

About this project

Wildzoekers was developed as part of the TU/e Industrial Design Final Bachelor Project by Mats van Deijck.

This project investigates how design can improve public perception and acceptance of wild nature in an urban environment.
